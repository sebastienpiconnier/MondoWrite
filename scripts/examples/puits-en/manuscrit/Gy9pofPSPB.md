You cross the yard without a light. You know this yard by heart. Or you used to.

On the third step your shin hits the trough. On the fifth your hand brushes the bark of the walnut tree, closer than you remember, as if the tree had moved during all these years. The gravel crunches. Then it doesn’t: you are walking on damp, soft earth that has no business being there in a month of drought.

At last your fingers find the rim. The stone is icy and wet, as if it were sweating. Following it with your fingertips, you feel a notch. Then another. A letter, clumsily scratched with the point of a nail or a stone: **P**.

Paul signed everything that way. His drawings, his exercise books, the wood of his bed.

Behind you, over by the workshop, someone laughs.

A short little laugh, muffled in a hand. You turn round. Under the lean-to, on the workbench, the lantern has just lit. By itself. The flame is tiny, blue at its base, and it sways gently, as if someone had just set it down.

No one is there.
