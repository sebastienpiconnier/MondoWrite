The water is cold. Not cold like a river in winter: cold like the absence of anything at all.

It closes over you without a sound. You don’t sink, you descend, gently, and there is no more up or down. Your light, if you had one, goes out without a hiss. The dark is complete, and yet you can see. You see the pale little lights turning around you. They come closer, curious, and one of them has the face of a boy in short trousers, shaking his head sadly.

You try to scream. What comes out of your mouth isn’t a scream. It is counting. *One. Two. Three.* You count, because once you have counted something, it can’t scare you any more.

You count for a very long time.

Then one day, or one night, there is no longer any difference, a light appears high above you. A grey circle. A small head leaning over the rim. A little girl, this time. The buyers had two children.

“Is anyone there?” she asks.

And you hear yourself answer, in a voice that is no longer quite yours, a soft voice, patient, affectionate:

“You took ages.”
