The rim is cold. An iron ladder goes down into the dark, and you can hear water far below.

A ladder. You played around this well a hundred times as a child, and you never saw a ladder. Yet the rungs are there, set into the stone, eaten by rust, as if they had always been there. They sink into a darkness so thick it seems to have a surface.

The air rising from it is warm. It smells of wet stone, and of something else: a sweet, slightly sickly smell, of apples forgotten at the bottom of a basket.

On the inside of the lid, leaning against the rim, the wood is scratched. Hundreds of small marks, close together, regular. They look like the marks you make to count days.

Far below, water drips. Then, between two drops, you think you hear something else. A rubbing sound. A breath being held, exactly like yours.
