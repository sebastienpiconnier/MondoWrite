You walk up to the stone column. It rises straight to the sky of water, and at its base, at ground level, there is a round hole. The bottom of the well. Or its mouth. Here, the two are the same thing.

You lean over. You look in.

You see the yard. The real one. From above, as if from a window. It is night, a summer night, hot, full of crickets. The moon lights the walnut tree and the rim. The lid doesn’t exist yet.

A little boy in striped pyjamas crosses the yard. He is carrying his sandals. He walks to the well without hurrying, his head tilted a little, like someone listening to a song.

And up at the window of the children’s bedroom, there is a face. A ten-year-old face, pressed to the glass. Yours.

You see yourself. You see yourself watch Paul set his sandals on the rim, side by side, neat and straight. You see yourself hesitate. You see yourself go back to bed, pull the sheet over your head, and decide very hard that you are asleep.

You had forgotten.

No. That isn’t true. You had *decided* to forget. You decided it that night, and decided it again every day for twenty-three years, so hard that it had become true.

Your chest tightens until it hurts. But in the middle of the pain there is something else, something lighter. Like a door you no longer need to hold shut.

At the far end of the yard, a field of grey wheat stretches down to the water.
