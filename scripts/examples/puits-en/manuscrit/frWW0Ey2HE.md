The barn door is open. Inside it is warm, and the smell of hay is so strong, so real, that it brings tears to your eyes.

The animals are there.

Not today’s: the farm has had none for a long time. The ones from before. The two white goats that “ran away” the winter you were eight. The old carthorse you only know from the photo in the hallway. Hens, rabbits, a ginger cat. They stand still, upright, eyes open, and their eyes catch the light like glass marbles. They are not sleeping. They are waiting.

Only one moves.

A thin yellow dog with a broken ear gets up from the pile of straw and comes towards you, wagging its tail very gently, as if not sure it is allowed to. Fanfan. Odile’s dog, who disappeared the first winter after Paul. Odile used to say he had gone to look for him.

He puts his nose in your hand. It is cold, but with a living coldness.

Against the wall, on a shelf, a can of paraffin. Full.

Fanfan tugs at your sleeve, towards the back of the barn, where a door you have never seen opens onto a field of grey wheat. He knows where Paul is. He has spent twenty-three years beside him.
