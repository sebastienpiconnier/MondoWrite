You are in the farmyard.

The same yard. The walnut tree, the trough, the leaning barn, the house with green shutters, the lean-to of the workshop. Everything is there, in its place, to the centimetre. But nothing makes a sound. Your steps don’t crunch on the gravel. The weathervane turns without creaking.

And there is no sky.

Instead of a sky, very high up, stretches a surface of black water, seen from below. It ripples slowly. The stars you saw earlier when you leant over the water at the bottom are reflections trembling on that surface, thousands of tiny lights that break apart and come together again. You are under the water of the well. You are *under* everything.

The light comes from everywhere and nowhere, the grey light before dawn, and it casts no shadows. Except yours. Yours is the only shadow in the yard, and it seems to be in the way.

In the middle of the yard, exactly where the well should be, a stone column rises towards the sky of water. The well, upside down. Its rim is up there, pressed against the surface.

Somewhere, very far off, a child’s voice is humming a nursery rhyme.

You feel, without knowing how, that this place will not let you see everything. Time, here, is something you drink, and there is little left in your glass. You will only be able to make one detour.
