The key turns without resistance, as if the lock had been waiting.

There are only two things in the drawer: a black leather notebook, swollen with damp, and an envelope with your name on it. Odile’s handwriting. You open the envelope.

*If you are reading this, I am dead, and the well is open.*

*I never told you anything because I thought it would die with me. I was wrong about a great many things.*

*Under the farm there is something very old and very thirsty. My great-grandfather made a bargain with it during the great drought: the Combes would never run short of water, and in return, in every generation, a child of the house would go down. Of their own accord. It never takes anyone, you understand. It calls, and you go down.*

*In 1958 it was my turn. I was nine. I heard the voice, I was afraid, and my brother Marcel went in my place. He said to me: “It’s nothing, I’ll be back.”*

*When the farm became mine, I swore no child would ever go down again. I had the lid put on. And then, on the night of 14 August 2003, I fell asleep in the armchair. A single night in forty years.*

*Paul is not dead. Down there, time does not pass. He is still seven. Ever since, every night, I have sat on that lid, so that it could not call you.*

*Its name is in the notebook. It can be bound by saying the name, at the bottom, in front of it. But a name has a price, and you must have light.*

*And above all: never give it yours.*

*I love you. Forgive me for the slap.*

The notebook is full of drawings: the farm, again and again, but upside down, with a sky that ripples like water. On the last page, a single word, in capitals, underlined three times: **OURDAS**.

You slip the notebook into your pocket.
