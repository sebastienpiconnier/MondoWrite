The workshop smells of linseed oil and sawdust. The lantern makes the shadows of the tools hanging from the beams dance, and for a moment the whole workshop seems to move with you.

On the big nail by the door, a hemp rope is coiled in a crown. Fifteen metres at least, as thick as a thumb. One end has been cut clean, recently: the fibres are still blond.

Above the workbench, a key board. Each key has a pencilled label in Odile’s slanting hand: *barn*, *cellar*, *henhouse*, *well padlock*. The padlock hook is empty. Next to it hangs a small blackened iron key, labelled *desk*.

And then there is the calendar.

A post office calendar from 2003, pinned to the wall, never replaced. August is still showing. The 14th is circled in red. But below it, on a sheet of brown paper taped on, Odile kept going: *2004, 2005, 2006…* A column of years down to this one. A small cross beside each. The last one dated last summer.

Under the column, one sentence, in a hand that was already shaking:

*As long as I sit on it, he can’t come up.*
