The door opens without a creak. The golden light you saw through the gap has gone: behind it there is only a narrow corridor with a low ceiling, whose walls are made of roots.

Enormous roots, knotted, laced together like fingers. You know them by their bitter smell, the smell of walnut husks that stains your hands for days. The walnut tree in the yard. You are walking under it, or rather inside it, through what it has been hiding underground for two hundred years.

The roots whisper.

Not in words, at first. A rustle, like silk being unfolded. Then, as you go on, you make out first names. *Étienne. Jeanne. Marcel.* Repeated softly, tenderly, the way you call a child in for supper.

And then, once, barely audible: your own name.

You stop. The whispering stops too. It is waiting to see whether you will answer.

You don’t answer. You walk on.

At the end of the corridor stands a second door. Green, with an iron latch, a cat flap and a scrape at hip height. Exactly the same as the first. You have a very clear feeling of having passed through something, but you couldn’t say whether it was a corridor, a mirror, or the years.
