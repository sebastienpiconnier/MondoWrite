Around the thirtieth rung, the wall hollows out. A niche, the size of a bread oven, carved into the black stone.

It is full of things.

A tin soldier with chipped paint. A blue satin ribbon, carefully rolled, and on a scrap of yellowed card: *Jeanne*. A wooden spinning top. A rusty harmonica. A porcelain doll’s head with a cracked gaze. Marbles, knucklebones, a whistle cut from an elder branch.

The treasures of the children who came down before you. They left them here on their way past, the way you leave your shoes on a doorstep.

And right at the front, a little apart, a big clear glass marble with a red spiral running through it. The dragon’s eye. Paul’s treasure. He never once lent it to you, not even when you cried for it. He said he would give it to you “when you’re grown up”, and that was the worst thing he could say, because you were already three years older than him.

You reach out.

As your fingers close on the marble, something else brushes them. A small warm hand, which at once draws back into the shadow of the niche. No cry. No fear. Just a gesture, as if to say: go on, take it.
