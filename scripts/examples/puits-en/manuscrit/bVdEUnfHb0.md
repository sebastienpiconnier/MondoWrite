“**OURDAS**.”

The name burns your mouth on the way out, like a mouthful of spirits that are too strong. It costs you something, you can feel it: a little breath, a little warmth, perhaps a few years. You will never know how much.

The thing freezes. All its voices scream at once, then fall silent. The standing water trembles, cracks like ice, and begins to shrink. It is no longer as tall as the barn, but as tall as a man, then a dog, then a puddle. Something very old, very thirsty and very small, deep down. Just a thirst that had learnt to speak.

The little lights come away from it.

One by one, they rise. Étienne. Jeanne. Marcel, who waves to you as he goes by. They float up to the sky of water, pass through it without disturbing it, and disappear.

Paul becomes a light too.

You hold out your arms. But he hasn’t recognised you, or not enough, or not in time, or you no longer had enough light for him to see you. He looks at you for a moment with polite curiosity, the way you look at a kind grown-up outside the school gates, and he goes with the others. You don’t know where. Somewhere you no longer need to count.

You climb back up without him. The ladder is shorter than on the way down. As you step over the rim, dawn is turning the walnut tree pink. You lean over: at the bottom of the well there is no more water. Just a circle of cracked earth, where a tiny blade of grass is already growing.

For the first time in a hundred and fifty years, the Combes well is dry.

You don’t know if you have won. You only know that it is over.

**THE END.** *There is an ending where Paul comes back up.*
