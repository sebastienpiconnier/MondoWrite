“Take me instead of him.”

You didn’t know you were going to say it. It came out on its own, with a simplicity that surprises you, like a sentence that had been waiting behind your teeth for twenty-three years.

The thing falls silent. All its voices fall silent at once.

“A child of the house,” it says at last, in Jeanne’s voice.

“I was one. I still am. Nobody ever told me I was allowed to stop.”

A long moment passes. Then the standing water bows, very slowly, the way you make a curtsey. Bargain struck.

Paul looks at you without understanding. You lift him up and carry him to the foot of the ladder. He is light, so light. You explain: forty rungs, the twenty-third is broken, you step over it. You tell him to go and knock at the Merciers’, the house at the end of the lane, and to say his name, his real full name. You tell him it will be strange at first. That everything will have changed. That he will have to be brave.

“Braver than you?” he asks.

You laugh. It is the first time in a long while.

You watch him climb. He counts aloud. At twenty-three, he steps over. At forty, his small figure stands out against a circle of sky that is already growing pale. He turns round, waves, and is gone.

In the kitchen below, Marcel has laid a fourth plate. He pulls out the chair for you.

“You’ll see,” he says. “You get used to it. And from down here, you can hear everything.”

High above you, very far away, Paul’s new life is beginning. You will listen to him grow up.

**THE END.**
