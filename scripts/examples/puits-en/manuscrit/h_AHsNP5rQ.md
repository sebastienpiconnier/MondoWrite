You kneel in front of him, so that your eyes are level.

And you tell him the truth. That you saw him cross the yard that night. That you saw him put his sandals on the rim. That you didn’t move. That you pulled the sheet over your head and pretended to be asleep, so hard you ended up believing it. That you have regretted it every day since, even the days you didn’t remember.

Your voice shakes. You don’t stop.

Paul stares at you. He says nothing for a long while. Then he wrinkles his nose, exactly like before, when he had just caught you out.

“I knew you were pretending. You breathe too loud.”

He slips his hand into yours. It is small and cold, and it doesn’t let go.

Behind him, the lake rises.
