You climb over the rim. The first rung groans under your weight, then falls silent.

If you have the rope, you tie one end to the pulley beam and the other round your waist. A bowline, the knot Odile taught you for tethering calves. Your hands remember it better than you do.

If you have the lantern, you hook it on your belt. Its light shows the walls up close: first bricks covered in moss, then bare stone, then a black, glistening stone unlike anything you know.

You go down. You count too, without meaning to. Ten. Fifteen. The circle of sky above is no more than a grey coin.

Around the twentieth rung, the wind picks up.

A wind that rises. It comes up from the bottom, warm and steady, like a breath out. Then it stops. Then it comes back. You understand, with a shiver that runs right down to your fingers, that the well is breathing, and that you are climbing down its throat. The draught flattens the lantern’s flame, which takes a long time to stand up again, smaller than before.

Twenty-one. Twenty-two.

Your foot feels for the next rung.
