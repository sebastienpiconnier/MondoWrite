You open your hand.

The dragon’s eye rolls in your palm, and the red spiral flares in the grey light, as if it had been waiting for this moment to wake up.

Paul freezes. The pebble slips from his fingers.

He looks at the marble. Then at you. Then at the marble again. For a long time. You watch something very slow and very complicated cross his face: a child working out a sum too big for him.

“I told you I’d give it to you when you were grown up,” he murmurs.

And he throws himself at you, so hard you nearly fall over backwards. He smells of hay, of wet stone, and beneath all that, faintly, of Odile’s washing powder.

Behind him, the lake rises.
