It says your name.

In your voice. Your real grown-up voice, the one you hear on voicemail and don’t like. It wears it the way you put on a coat, settling the shoulders.

You open your mouth to answer. Nothing comes out. Not a breath, not a whisper. Your voice is over there, in the standing water, and it goes on talking without you.

“Thank you,” it says. “It’s so much simpler when you give it to us.”

The water shrinks, gathers itself, settles. It takes a shape. Yours. Your height, your way of standing a little crooked, your coat, your shoes. It turns to the ladder and begins to climb, counting the rungs aloud in your voice, cheerfully.

You run after it. The green door shuts in front of you. The latch won’t move.

Paul takes your hand. He says nothing either. He understood before you did, as always.

The next morning, at the notary’s, someone who looks exactly like you signs the deed of sale with a smile. They shake hands with the buyers, a young couple with two children. They tell them the farm is wonderful for little ones, that there is room to play, and a very old well, full of water that never runs dry.

They leave them the key to the padlock.
