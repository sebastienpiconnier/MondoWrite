You put the lid back. It is heavier than you remember, and for a second, as you slide it across, you feel something pushing from the other side. You close the padlock. The click echoes across the whole yard.

In the house, you don’t light anything. You climb the stairs counting the steps without meaning to, the way you and Paul used to, to see who would reach the top first. Thirteen. Your childhood bedroom hasn’t changed. The bunk beds. The faded poster. The bolt on the inside, which Odile had fitted the winter after.

You slide the bolt and go to bed. That night you dream of a ladder that never ends.

You wake at twelve minutes past three. You know because the kitchen clock still strikes once at that hour, for no reason, and has for years.

Someone is coming up the stairs. Light steps, bare feet. A small voice counts, very softly: “… eleven… twelve… thirteen.”

Something scratches at the door. Gently. Patiently.

“I know you’re not asleep,” says the voice. “You’re breathing like when you pretended.”

You close your eyes. You breathe slowly. You pretend to be asleep, exactly as you did twenty-three years ago, the night you heard the door downstairs open and didn’t move.

The scratching goes on until dawn.

The next day, at the notary’s, you sign without reading. You will never go back to the Combes. But some nights, at twelve minutes past three, wherever you are, someone is counting steps outside your door.
