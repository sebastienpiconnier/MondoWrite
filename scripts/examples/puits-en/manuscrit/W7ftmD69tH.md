At the bottom of the well, a door is set into the stone. It is ajar.

The fortieth rung stops a metre above a ledge of dry stone. You jump. The ground is firm, almost warm. At your feet the water begins: black water, perfectly still, so smooth you would take it for a slab of slate. It reflects nothing. Not the lantern, not your face. Only, very far inside it, pale points that look like stars.

The door is made of wood. A real farmhouse door, painted green, with an iron latch and a cat flap cut out at the bottom. It is the door of Odile’s kitchen. You recognise the scrape at hip height that Paul made with his bike.

Through the gap comes a golden light, impossible, the light of a late summer afternoon. And a smell of leek soup. Odile made it on Sundays.

To the right of the door, the wall is covered in carvings: strokes, letters, dates, scratched at a child’s height.

You lay your hand on the wood. It is warm.
