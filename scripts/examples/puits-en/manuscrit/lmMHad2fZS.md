You say nothing. You hold your breath and listen.

For a long time there is only the water. One drop. Another. Then, far below, a sound of metal, a faint ring, like a fingernail against a rung. And a child’s voice, whispering to itself:

“… nineteen… twenty… twenty-one… twenty-two…”

A pause.

“Twenty-three, careful, it’s broken, step over it… twenty-four…”

You know this game. Paul counted everything. Stairs, cobblestones, cows in the field, the seconds between lightning and thunder. He said that once you have counted something, it can’t scare you any more.

The voice goes on, flat, all the way to “forty”. Then a small splash, a pebble dropped in. A laugh.

And suddenly, very close, as if the mouth were just below the rim:

“I know you’re there. You’re breathing like when you pretended to be asleep.”

You jerk back. Your heart pounds. You have never told anyone about that.
