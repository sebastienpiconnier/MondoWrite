You lift the lantern. The flame wavers, but it holds.

The yellow light pushes the shadows back a few steps. It shows the lean-to, the sacks of grain, and the tool wall. Odile had drawn the outline of every tool there in chalk, so she could tell at a glance which one was missing. Scythe, billhook, saw, hammer: everything is in its place.

Except one. At the very bottom of the wall, at a child’s height, an empty outline: a little spade with a short handle. Paul’s spade. The one he used to dig “tunnels for the ants” at the foot of the walnut tree.

You lower the lantern towards the workbench. There are marks in the dust. Palms, tiny fingers. As if a child had climbed up there to reach the lantern, then put it back, not knowing how to light it.

The marks are fresh.

Behind you, the workshop door gapes onto the dark. In front of you, the yard, and the white rim of the well catching the light.
