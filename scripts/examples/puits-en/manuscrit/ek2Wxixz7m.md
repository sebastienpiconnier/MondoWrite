The lit window is the kitchen’s. You push the green door, for the third time.

A boy is laying the table.

Nine, perhaps ten. Grey wool shorts, socks falling down, a side parting combed with water. He sets the plates out with enormous care: one, two, three, four. He looks up at you, and you recognise Odile’s eyes. The same grey, the same way of frowning before he smiles.

“You must be family,” he says. “You’ve got her nose. I’m Marcel.”

He doesn’t ask your name. You notice he is very careful not to ask it.

“Odile’s dead, isn’t she?” It isn’t really a question. “I felt it. The lid went light.”

He sits down, and while he talks his hands keep smoothing the tablecloth.

“She was frightened that night. She was shaking so much she couldn’t get her clogs on. So I went. I don’t regret it. She had a life, you see? A real one. You, for instance.”

Then he leans towards you and lowers his voice.

“The one who drinks, his name is **OURDAS**. If you say his name in front of him, he has to listen. But you need light, or he’ll drink you before you’ve finished. And Paul…” He hesitates. “Paul won’t follow you if he doesn’t recognise you. You’ve changed a lot. To him, you’re still ten.”

He sets down a fourth spoon, in front of the empty chair.

“That one’s for you. Just in case.”
