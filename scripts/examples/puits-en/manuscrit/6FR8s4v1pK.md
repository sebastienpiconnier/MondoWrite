You bring the light close to the wall.

These are not random marks. They are names. Each carved by a different hand, at a different height, with whatever was to hand: a nail, a belt buckle, a sharp stone.

**ÉTIENNE 1874.** The letters are wide, careful, almost elegant.

**JEANNE 1903.** With a little flower beside it.

**MARCEL 1958.** And below, smaller: *for Odile*.

**PAUL 2003.** The P is backwards, the way he always wrote it when he was in a hurry.

Around the names there are strokes. Thousands of strokes, in groups of five, covering the stone right up to the ceiling. Counted days. Years of days. You try not to imagine how long it takes to fill a wall like that.

And then, between the names, another word keeps coming back. Carved by each child in turn, then crossed out, scribbled over, scratched until it has almost vanished, as if they had learnt it by heart and been afraid of having written it. You trace the letters with your finger.

**OURDAS.**

As you make it out, you feel the well hold its breath.

You stay too long. The light dims.
